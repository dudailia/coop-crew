# Anti-slop catalogue

coop-crew's own list of the tells that make outgoing copy read as machine-written, written for this repo (MIT, like
the rest of it). Rule numbers are stable: cite a rule as "catalogue 7", never renumber, and add new rules at the end.

Strength: **strong** means one sighting justifies an edit. **Weak** means edit only when other tells sit near it.
Every fix keeps the claim. If removing a tell would drop a fact, a hedge or a condition, rewrite around it instead.

## Structure

1. **Staged contrast (strong).** "It's not X, it's Y", "not just X but Y", "less about X, more about Y" when nobody
   said X. Say Y. If X is the author's own claim, keep both: "I built X and led Y".
2. **Run-up before the point (strong).** "Here's the thing:", "The truth is", "Let me be clear", "What this means is".
   Start with the point.
3. **Slogan closer (strong).** A last line that restates the paragraph as a motto ("That's the power of data.",
   "And that makes all the difference."). Cut it. End on the last real claim or the ask.
4. **Reflex triad (weak).** Three parallel items where the third adds nothing ("fast, reliable and scalable") or every
   list has exactly three. Keep the items that carry a claim.
5. **Question then answer (weak).** "The result? A 40% drop." Make it a plain sentence: "The drop was 40%." Keep the
   number exactly as written.
6. **Verdict header (weak).** A bold label that announces a judgement before the text ("**The bottom line:**",
   "**Why it matters:**") in an email or answer. Remove the label, keep the sentence.
7. **Equal-length rhythm (weak).** A run of sentences of the same length and shape. Vary only by merging or splitting
   where the meaning allows; never pad.

## Words

8. **Hype words (strong in copy).** passionate, thrilled, excited to, cutting-edge, seamless, robust, game-changing,
   world-class, innovative, revolutionize, synergy, leverage (as a verb), unlock, empower, elevate, supercharge.
   Replace with the plain claim, or drop the word.
9. **Inflated significance (strong).** "plays a pivotal role", "a testament to", "stands as", "underscores the
   importance of", "in today's fast-paced world", "ever-evolving landscape". State what happened.
10. **Stock model vocabulary (weak).** delve, tapestry, intricate, multifaceted, nuanced, realm, navigate (for anything
    but routes), foster, bolster, showcase, meticulous, holistic, embark, journey (for a job). Use the everyday word.
11. **Flat-certainty adverbs (weak).** truly, genuinely, incredibly, deeply, undeniably, fundamentally, simply. Cut
    them unless the sentence changes meaning without them.
12. **Stacked hedges (strong).** "I believe it might possibly help". Keep one hedge if the author meant one.

## Punctuation and format

13. **Em dashes (strong).** No em dashes in outgoing copy. Use a comma, a period, a colon, or parentheses around a full
    grammatical unit. The author's own writing sample overrides this at the author's own rate.
14. **Bold scattered through prose (weak).** Bold on phrases inside an email or answer. Remove it; the portal or the
    reader's mail client is not a slide.
15. **Bullets where a sentence works (weak).** Two or three bullets in a short email. Write the sentence. Resumes keep
    their bullets.
16. **Emoji and decorative symbols (strong in email and answers).** Remove them unless the author uses them.
17. **Title Case Headings in emails (weak).** Emails have no headings.

## Chatbot residue

18. **Assistant openers and closers (strong).** "Great question", "Certainly!", "I hope this helps", "Feel free to
    reach out if you have any questions", "Let me know if you'd like me to". Cut them; keep one real ask.
19. **Talking about the text (strong).** "In this email I will", "As mentioned above", "To summarize". Say the thing.
20. **Placeholder residue (strong).** "[Company Name]", "Dear Hiring Manager" when a name is known, "insert X here".
    Fill from the facts or ask; never guess a name.
21. **Re-explaining what the reader knows (weak).** Telling a recruiter what their own posting says at length. One
    clause that shows you read it is enough.

## Claims and tone

22. **Vague attribution (strong).** "Experts say", "studies show", "many believe" with no source. Cut it or ask the
    author for the source. Never add one.
23. **Generic praise of the company (weak).** "I admire your commitment to innovation". Replace with the specific fact
    from the brief that made the author interested, or cut it. Add no fact that is not in the brief or the posting.
24. **Sales register (strong in applications).** "I would be a perfect fit", "I'm confident I can add immense value",
    "hit the ground running". Replace with the evidence the author has, from `profile/facts.md`.
25. **Overclaiming verbs (weak).** spearheaded, revolutionized, transformed, pioneered, when the facts say "built" or
    "helped". Use the verb the facts support.
26. **Invented specifics (hard lock 1).** Any number, name, date or quote not in the source. This is not a style tell;
    it is a false claim. Remove it and tell the author.

## Overcorrection (what not to do)

27. **Do not flatten the author's voice.** A plain sentence that the author wrote stays, even if it trips a weak rule.
28. **Do not cut a claim with its shape.** When you remove a staged form, the claim inside it stays.
29. **Do not swap one tell for another.** Replacing every dash with a semicolon, or every "leverage" with "utilize",
    is the same tell.
30. **The right edit to a clean draft is no edit.**
